Every clock app can wake you up Monday to Friday. Almost none of them can handle 4 on, 2 off, 3 on, 3 off — so you end up re-doing your alarms every single week, or leaving fifteen half-wrong ones on and hoping you toggle the right ones before bed.

xalarm was built to fix exactly that.

Set your rotation once and you're done. Pick a built-in pattern — Panama (2-2-3), DuPont, Pitman, 4-on/4-off, Southern Swing — or tap out your own cycle on a day grid, anywhere from 2 to 42 days. Tell it which date is day 1 of your rotation and the app works out the rest, forever. The alarm list always shows exactly when the next one fires, so you can check tonight's situation at a glance.

It handles the messy details real schedules have:

- Different start times on different days of the same rotation. Days at 6am, nights at 6pm, a later start on your first day back — whatever your hitch looks like.
- Alarms that begin on a certain date, stop on a certain date, or ring a set number of times and then shut off. Covering someone for a week? Set it once, it stops on its own.
- Every 12 hours from a start time. Every third day. The third Tuesday of the month. If your schedule can describe it, the alarm can follow it.
- A home screen widget with your next alarm and the coming week of your rotation — filled squares are work days, so you can answer "am I on Saturday?" without opening anything.

The everyday stuff is all here too, and it works how you'd expect: a world clock that knows real cities (San Antonio and Dallas, not just zone names), a stopwatch that keeps counting even if you restart your phone, a timer you can start with a tap on the dial, plus per-alarm ringtones with your own sound files and a hold-to-pick snooze (5, 10, 30, or type your own).

There's also a zen focus timer: an hourglass with actual falling sand. Flip your phone over and the sand runs backward to give yourself more time. Set it on its side and it pauses, like a real hourglass would. When the glass empties, you get a gentle buzz and a nudge to stand up and move around.

And if you and a partner want the same countdown — timing something together from two places — you can pair two phones with a short code and share a live timer and stopwatch either of you can control. The connection is obvious on screen the whole time, and everything about it is erased the moment you disconnect.

No accounts. No ads. No tracking. Your alarms and schedules stay on your phone.

Built by a shift worker who got tired of fighting normal alarm apps. If your schedule is the reason you found this page, this was made for you.
